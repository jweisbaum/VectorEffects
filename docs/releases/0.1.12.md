# VectorEffects 0.1.12 Beta

This update lets you lay a picture on the map by pointing at things: click a
place in the picture, click the same place on the chart, and the picture bends
to fit. It also reads GPX, zooms eleven times closer, and fixes a handful of
things the layer panel was saying that were not true.

**This beta expires on January 1, 2027.** Install a newer version before that
date to continue using VectorEffects.

`SHA256SUMS` contains checksums for the downloadable installers and app archives.
Mac builds without Developer ID credentials use ad-hoc signing without
notarization. Windows installers are unsigned; the operating system may require
approval before launch.

## New

- **Align a picture by pointing at it.** An image layer now has an align button
  beside its opacity. Press it, then click a feature in the picture and the
  place it belongs on the chart — a headland, a light, a pier head — as many
  times as you like, and press **Enter**. Every pair you place lands exactly
  where you put it. **Escape** cancels, **Backspace** drops the last pair.

  What happens between the pairs depends on how many you place. One moves the
  picture. Two also turn and scale it. Three shear it. Four correct a
  perspective — a chart photographed at an angle, or a screenshot taken off
  square. Five or more *bend* it, so a scanned paper chart can be pulled onto
  the map despite its own projection and the paper's own distortion, which no
  amount of rotating and stretching will fix. Up to fifty pairs.

  This works in every projection, the globe included.

- **Drag a picture to line it up.** With the hand tool, dragging inside the
  active image layer moves the whole picture; dragging anywhere else pans the
  map as before. Any alignment you have already placed travels with it, so the
  picture stays put relative to the marks you pinned.

- **GPX.** *Import GIS* now reads `.gpx` alongside shapefiles, GeoJSON and
  KML/KMZ: waypoints, routes and recorded tracks. A track that lost its fix
  keeps its gap rather than drawing a leg that was never sailed.

## Changed

- **The map zooms eleven times closer.** The old limit stopped at about 217 m a
  pixel, which was too far out to read a berthing chart or an OpenStreetMap
  street at the scale either was drawn for. It now goes to about 19 m a pixel —
  where the map's own tiles run out, and not before.

- **The cursor readout follows the timeline.** During playback it used to keep
  showing the step it was last sampled at, disagreeing with the map underneath
  it. It now updates with each frame.

- **Lines and marks in a GIS layer are no longer filled.** A route, a track or a
  coastline is a path, so the fill opacity does not reach it, and the fill
  control only appears when the file holds an area to fill. A mark is a dot in
  the layer's colour rather than a ring of its fill.

- **The layer panel says less, and means it.** A long file name is truncated
  instead of pushing the move and delete buttons out of the panel. Display-only
  layers — images and GIS files — no longer offer a speed filter, since they
  make no field to threshold. An imported layer no longer describes itself as
  empty while holding a file.

## Known issues

- A picture that straddles the date line is drawn folded under Robinson and the
  other fixed world projections. The flat map and the globe are unaffected. This
  is long-standing and not specific to pictures — anything crossing 180° in
  those projections is affected.
